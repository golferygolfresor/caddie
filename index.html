Distances by club

Enter your average 7-iron carry. Every other club is estimated from the typical gaps between clubs. As you get more consistent, update the number and the whole bag moves with it.

    The 7-iron is the anchor because it sits in the middle of the bag and is the club most beginners hit most often. Rough numbers are fine — your own dispersion is bigger than the estimate error. When you know a real distance for another club, type it over the estimate and it is kept.

    Drills for consistent contact

    Distance is a by-product of clean contact. Work on these in this order; don't chase yards until strikes are steady.

    1. Towel behind the ball

    Lay a folded towel about 15 cm behind the ball. Hit half-swing 8-irons without touching the towel. This kills the fat shot and teaches you to strike ball first, then turf.

    2. Nine-to-three swings

    Hands go to 9 o'clock on the backswing, 3 o'clock on the follow-through. 20 balls with a 9-iron. Most of your swing faults show up in the long swing — the short swing fixes the sequence.

    3. Feet-together drill

    Stand with feet touching and hit 3/4 shots. If you lose balance you are swinging with your arms and shoulders instead of turning. Balance first, speed later.

    4. One club, one target, 10 balls

    Same club, same target, and count good strikes out of 10. That number is what you log in Progress. It matters more than how far the best one went.

    5. Alignment sticks

    One stick on the ground along your feet, one at the target line. Beginners aim 10–20 m right without knowing it and then "fix" it with a bad swing.

    Session shape: 10 balls warm-up with a wedge, 30 balls on one drill, 20 balls to a target with your 7-iron (this is your logging number), 10 wedges to finish. About 70 balls — more than that and quality drops.
